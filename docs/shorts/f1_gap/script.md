## F1-7. The corner you can't see (Explained · voice: Malachi; Bernard dropped 21 Oct)

**Facts, checked 21 Oct:** Turn 1 at the Circuit of the Americas climbs more than 30 metres from the start line (formula1.com; PlanetF1 gives 133 ft) at about 11 percent (Racer). It's a first-gear hairpin and the apex is blind on the way up. The track is very wide at the entry. Sources: formula1.com, Racer, PlanetF1.

### Narration

This is the most dramatic first corner in Formula One. And the drivers can't see it.

[COTA Turn 1 from the start line: the wall of tarmac going up.]

At the start in Austin, twenty cars charge straight up a hill. They climb more than thirty metres. That's a ten-storey building, in a few hundred metres of track.

[Elevation graphic: the profile rising, a ten-storey block beside it for scale.]

Going uphill helps the brakes. Gravity slows the car too, so drivers can brake later than they could on the flat.

But at the top, the road just disappears. It's a hairpin, taken in first gear, and the apex is hidden behind the crest.

[Driver's-eye graphic: sky where the corner should be, then the hairpin appearing.]

So the drivers aim at a point they can't see, and trust it's there.

And it's built very wide, on purpose, so cars can fan out and pass on the way in.

Watch the start on Sunday. Count how many places change at the top of the hill.

## F1-8. The race with six cars (Did you know · voice: Kirk)

**Facts, checked 21 Oct:** United States Grand Prix, Indianapolis, 19 June 2005. Michelin found its tyres could fail through the banked Turn 13 after Ralf Schumacher's crash in Friday practice. The 14 Michelin cars pulled into the pits after the formation lap; only the 6 Bridgestone cars (Ferrari, Jordan, Minardi) raced. Michael Schumacher won from Rubens Barrichello; Tiago Monteiro (Jordan) was third, his only F1 podium. The crowd booed. Indianapolis held its last F1 race in 2007. Sources: Wikipedia, Motorsport Magazine, RaceFans, Autosport.

### Narration

This is the only Formula One race where just six cars started.

[Card: 20 CARS ON THE GRID. The number drops to 6.]

Indianapolis, 2005. The United States Grand Prix.

In practice, a car crashed hard in the banked last corner. Michelin, who made the tyres for most of the teams, found their tyres might not survive that banking at full speed. And they had no safe tyre to swap in.

[Graphic: the banked turn, a tyre sidewall flexing, a red warning.]

So on Sunday, all twenty cars did the formation lap. Then fourteen of them drove straight into the pits, and parked.

Six cars raced. Two Ferraris, two Jordans, two Minardis.

The crowd booed. Some threw bottles onto the track.

Michael Schumacher won. And a Jordan driver, Tiago Monteiro, finished third. The only podium of his career.

Two years later, Formula One left Indianapolis for good.

## F1-9. Champion on two wheels and four (On this day · voice: Tom)

**Facts, checked 21 Oct:** Mexican Grand Prix, 25 October 1964, the final round. John Surtees (Ferrari 158) finished second behind Dan Gurney and won the title by one point from Graham Hill, 40 to 39. Hill was knocked off by Surtees's team-mate Lorenzo Bandini; Jim Clark, leading, had an engine failure in the closing laps; Bandini let Surtees through to second. Ferrari raced in the blue and white of the North American Racing Team (NART) after a row with the Italian sporting authority. Surtees had won 7 motorcycle world titles with MV Agusta (500cc 1956, 1958, 1959, 1960; 350cc 1958, 1959, 1960). He's still the only world champion on two wheels and four. Sources: Wikipedia, Ferrari, Motor Sport (December 1964).

### Narration

Sixty-one years ago today, a motorbike racer became Formula One world champion.

[On screen: 25 OCTOBER 1964 · MEXICO CITY.]

John Surtees had already won seven world titles on motorbikes. Now, at the last race of 1964, he could win one in a car.

Three drivers could take the title. Surtees, Graham Hill and Jim Clark.

[Three-way points graphic: HILL 39, SURTEES and CLARK chasing.]

Hill was knocked off by Surtees's own team-mate. Then Clark, leading, had his engine fail in the closing laps.

And the team-mate let Surtees through to second. Enough to win the title by one point.

Oh, and his Ferrari wasn't red. It was blue and white. Enzo Ferrari had fallen out with the Italian racing authorities, so the cars raced in American colours.

[Photo: a blue-and-white Ferrari 158.]

Sixty-one years on, Surtees is still the only man to be world champion on two wheels and four.
