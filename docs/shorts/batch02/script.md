## F1-4. Why F1 tyres have no tread (Omnira F1)

Why do F1 tyres have no tread at all?

On a dry track, grip comes from rubber touching the road. Tread grooves only take rubber away. So a dry tyre is completely smooth. A slick.

But in the rain, that's a problem. A slick can't push water out of the way, so it rides up on top of it. That's aquaplaning.

So when it rains, teams switch to tyres with deep grooves.

A full wet tyre can clear around eighty-five litres of water every second at top speed. That's a full bath, every two seconds.

From 1998 to 2008, F1 even made teams use grooved tyres in the dry, to slow the cars down.

## F1-5. The 1.8-second pit stop (Omnira F1)

Four new tyres in under two seconds.

In 2023, at the Qatar Grand Prix, McLaren changed all four of Lando Norris's tyres in one point eight seconds. A world record.

[Counter: 0.0 to 1.80, crew dots lighting up.]

It takes around twenty people. Three on each wheel: one with the wheel gun, one pulling the old tyre off, one putting the new one on.

Then two on the jacks, front and rear, and others steadying the car.

They practise it hundreds of times a season.

And the driver? He just has to stop on his marks, exactly.

## F1-6. Could an F1 car drive upside down? (Omnira F1)

They say an F1 car could drive upside down. Could it really?

At speed, the wings and the floor push the car down onto the track. That's downforce.

And at high speed, the downforce becomes bigger than the weight of the car itself.

So in theory, on the roof of a tunnel, going fast enough, the downforce would hold it up there.

Engineers reckon you'd need somewhere around one hundred and fifty to two hundred kilometres an hour.

Nobody has ever tried it. But on paper, it works.

## FB-5. The goalkeeper with more than a hundred goals (Omnira Football)

This goalkeeper scored more than a hundred goals.

Rogério Ceni played for São Paulo, in Brazil, for over twenty years.

He took their free kicks. He took their penalties.

By the time he retired, he'd scored one hundred and thirty-one goals. More than many strikers manage in a whole career.

And he still kept goal.

## FB-6. Why is it called a hat-trick? (Omnira Football)

Why is three goals called a hat-trick?

It started in cricket. In 1858, a bowler called H. H. Stephenson took three wickets with three balls in a row.

The crowd held a collection, and with the money they bought him a hat.

Three in a row earned you a hat. A hat-trick.

Football borrowed it. And these days, the player who scores three gets to keep the match ball.

## Fact notes (for us, not for reading)

- **Tyres:** slicks in the dry. Grooved dry tyres were mandatory 1998–2008, slicks returned in 2009. Pirelli's figure for a full wet is about 85 L/s at 300 km/h (intermediates about 30 L/s). A bath is about 150–170 L, so "a full bath every two seconds" holds.
- **Pit stop:** McLaren, Norris, Qatar GP 2023, 1.80 s (the world record). Crew is about 20+.
- **Upside down:** a theoretical estimate that varies with the car. Keep "somewhere around" and "in theory".
- **Leicester:** 5000–1 before 2015/16. They survived relegation in 2014/15 after being bottom. Claudio Ranieri was appointed July 2015. The Elvis odds (2,000–1 at some bookmakers) were widely reported; keep it as "bookies thought it more likely". The squad-cost line is widely reported; consider "a team that cost less than one big transfer".
- **Ceni:** 131 goals (free kicks and penalties), São Paulo 1990–2015.
- **Hat-trick:** HH Stephenson, 1858, three wickets in three balls, given a hat bought with a collection (the usual account).
