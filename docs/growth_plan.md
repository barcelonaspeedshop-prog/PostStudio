# PostStudio growth plan (14 Oct 2026)

Goal: fewer, better videos that people watch to the end, then more channels once the system works.

## Why Ford vs Ferrari (GoF, April) got 80 views

- **Nothing new.** It's the most-told story in motorsport, with a Hollywood film and thousands of videos already. A new channel can't win the same story told the same way.
- **Too short to be good.** At 4 minutes there's no room for the detail that makes people stay and come back. It's a summary, not a story.
- **AI faces of real people.** Enzo Ferrari and Henry Ford II as AI portraits makes it look like a mass-produced AI slideshow. Viewers spot it in seconds, and so does YouTube.
- **Generic packaging.** "The battle that changed racing forever" could be any of 500 videos. The thumbnail is a poster, not a question.
- **No reason to subscribe.** No series, no promise of what comes next.

The 3DCrash video worked for the opposite reasons: one clear promise ("we'll show you exactly what happened, in 3D"), every technical point shown on screen, and a format nobody else does.

## Our angle: "the story, and how it actually worked"

Every episode tells a great story AND explains the engineering or tactic behind it, visually, so a casual fan understands it and an expert respects it. That's our 3D reconstruction: a signature **"how it works"** segment in every video (Brawn: the diffuser; fan car: the fan; Group B: why the cars were too fast; the back-pass rule: what changed on the pitch).

Rules for every video:
1. **Pick stories with an unknown angle.** If the story is famous, find the part people don't know. If people don't know the story, the title has to make them curious.
2. **Title and thumbnail first.** Write them before the script. If they aren't clickable, change the topic.
3. **First 30 seconds:** the most surprising fact, then a promise of what they'll learn ("the secret was hidden under the car").
4. **Explain every technical word with a picture within 10 seconds of saying it.** Real photo, then clean animated drawing, then the fun cartoon.
5. **Something changes on screen every 3–5 seconds.** No graphic held longer than 6 seconds.
6. **No AI faces of real people, ever.** AI only for places, cars, atmosphere and technical renders.
7. **8–12 minutes for long videos.** Long enough for detail, short enough to finish.
8. **Leave a question for the comments, and reply to every comment in the first 24 hours.**

## Cadence

- **Start with one long video a week**, per channel we're running. Each video goes to **one channel** (agreed 14 Oct), not three: the same video on three channels splits views, comments and subscribers three ways.
  - Omnira F1: F1 stories (best Shorts performer so far, start here)
  - Gentlemen of Fuel: wider motorsport and car stories
  - Omnira Football: football stories
  - Road & Trax: paused until before next season
- **Shorts every day** on each active channel, cut from that week's long video. One teaser Short the day before the long video; the rest go out after it, each with its Related video set to the full episode so viewers can click straight through.
- **No release dates until the content is finished.** Dates are set once a video is built and approved.
- **Built one week ahead.** Script written and checked → Mike narrates → build → Mike reviews → schedule. One video at a time.

## Measure every week (YouTube Studio)

- **Click-through rate** on the thumbnail: under 4% means the title or thumbnail needs changing (use Test & Compare for thumbnails).
- **Viewers still watching at 30 seconds:** under 60% means the opening needs fixing.
- **Average percentage viewed:** aim for 40%+ on long videos.
- **Shorts to subscribers:** which Shorts bring subscribers, not just views.

We look at these together every Monday and change one thing at a time.
