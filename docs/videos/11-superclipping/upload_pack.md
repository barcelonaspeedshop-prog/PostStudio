## Description

Bahrain, February 2026: a brand-new Formula One car comes out of the fastest corner flat out, and slows down by 30 km/h without the driver lifting. Within three months, F1 had to change its own brand-new rules.

This is superclipping, explained:
- why the 2026 cars are half petrol, half battery, and why the battery runs empty in seconds
- the motor on the rear axle that runs backwards as a generator
- lift and coast, the moving wings, and why harvesting at full throttle became the answer
- what it looked like: 267 down to 233 km/h in Bahrain, 16 seconds a lap in Melbourne
- the 50 km/h closing speed at Suzuka, and Oliver Bearman's 50G crash
- the fix before Miami (250 kW to 350 kW), and what the drivers made of it
- how to spot superclipping yourself on TV

Clever engineering, or a sign the rules went too far? Tell me in the comments.
