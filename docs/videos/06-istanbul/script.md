# IMPOSSIBLE: 3–0 Down at Half-Time in a Final

Script for narration, about 830 words, roughly 6 minutes at a relaxed pace.

Read it naturally and change any wording that doesn't sound like you. [Square brackets] are notes for the edit. Don't read them out.

After a mistake, say "stop", pause, and restart the whole sentence.

**Names:** only easy English names are spoken (Gerrard, Carragher, Kewell, Mellor, Finnan). Everyone else is described by role, and the edit puts their name on screen. **[On screen: …]** notes show where.

## 1. Cold open

At half-time in the 2005 Champions League final, Liverpool were losing three–nil against AC Milan, one of the best teams in the world.

Nobody comes back from that. Not in a final, and definitely not against Milan.

Fifteen minutes into the second half, the score was three–three.

This is the story of Liverpool's Champions League miracle in Istanbul.

**[Title card: IMPOSSIBLE. — "Istanbul 2005."]**

## 2. The outsiders

Liverpool shouldn't really have been there in the first place.

They had a new Spanish manager, in his first season at the club.

**[On screen: Rafael Benítez · Manager]**

In the Premier League, they finished fifth. Not even good enough to qualify for the next Champions League.

And in Europe, they nearly went out before Christmas.

In their last group game, at Anfield against the Greek champions, they had to win. And because they'd lost one–nil in Greece, if the Greeks scored, Liverpool would have to win by two clear goals.

**[On screen: Liverpool v Olympiacos · 8 December 2004]**

Then a Brazilian World Cup winner curled in a free kick. At half-time, Liverpool were losing one–nil. Now they needed three goals in forty-five minutes.

**[On screen: Rivaldo]**

The manager sent on a young French striker. Two minutes after the restart, he equalised. One–one.

**[On screen: Florent Sinama-Pongolle]**

With nine minutes left, another substitute, Neil Mellor, made it two–one. But that still wasn't enough.

Then, with four minutes to go, Mellor headed the ball down, and Steven Gerrard hit it first time from twenty-five yards, into the corner.

Three–one. Liverpool were through. Looking back, it was a sign of things to come.

After that, they knocked out a German side, then the Italian giants, then Chelsea.

**[On screen: Bayer Leverkusen · Juventus · Chelsea]**

And somehow, they were in the final.

## 3. The giants

So to the final. Waiting for Liverpool in Istanbul was a team that can only be described as a European football Goliath. AC Milan.

Champions of Europe two years earlier, and packed with world-class players.

Two of the best defenders on the planet. Two of the finest midfielders of their generation. And up front, the European Footballer of the Year.

**[On screen, one line per phrase: Maldini · Nesta / Pirlo · Kaká / Shevchenko · Crespo]**

The final was at the national stadium, out on the edge of Istanbul, on the twenty-fifth of May, 2005.

**[On screen: Atatürk Olympic Stadium · 25 May 2005]**

Liverpool in red. Milan in white.

## 4. The first half

It took Milan less than a minute to score.

A free kick came in, and the Milan captain volleyed it home. About fifty seconds on the clock.

**[On screen: Paolo Maldini · 0:50]**

It's still the fastest goal ever scored in a Champions League final.

Then Liverpool lost Harry Kewell to injury. A Czech midfielder came on in his place. Remember him.

**[On screen: Vladimír Šmicer]**

Six minutes before the break, Milan's star striker crossed, and their Argentinian forward tapped it in. Two–nil.

**[On screen: Shevchenko → Crespo]**

Right on half-time, Milan's young Brazilian playmaker split the Liverpool defence with one pass. The same Argentinian chipped the goalkeeper.

**[On screen: Kaká → Crespo]**

Three–nil to Milan at the break.

## 5. Half-time

Years later, the data company Opta worked out Liverpool's chances from there.

Just over half of one per cent.

**[ODDS graphic: 0.55% — "Opta win probability, 3–0 down".]**

In the dressing room, the manager didn't panic. He changed the plan.

A German midfielder came on for Steve Finnan. Liverpool switched to three at the back, and Gerrard was pushed further forward.

**[On screen: Dietmar Hamann on · Steve Finnan off]**

Meanwhile, up in the stands, the Liverpool fans did something remarkable.

They didn't boo. They sang. "You'll Never Walk Alone."

Some of the players say they could hear it from the dressing room. The manager says he only heard it on the way back out.

Either way, the fans hadn't given up. So why should the team?

## 6. Six minutes

Nine minutes into the second half, Liverpool's left-back crossed from the left.

**[On screen: John Arne Riise]**

Gerrard rose and headed it in. Three–one.

Two minutes later, the Czech substitute hit a low shot from outside the box. It skidded past the Milan keeper. Three–two.

**[On screen: Šmicer · past Dida]**

Then Gerrard was brought down in the box. Penalty.

**[On screen: fouled by Gattuso]**

Liverpool's Spanish midfielder stepped up. The keeper saved it.

**[On screen: Xabi Alonso]**

But the ball came straight back to him, and he smashed in the rebound.

Three goals in six minutes. Three–three.

**[ODDS graphic: 0.55% → 36.7% — "Opta win probability after the equaliser".]**

## 7. The save

After that, Milan took control again. Liverpool clung on into extra time.

With three minutes left, the ball fell to Milan's star striker, right in front of goal.

Liverpool's Polish goalkeeper blocked his first effort.

**[On screen: Jerzy Dudek v Andriy Shevchenko]**

The ball came straight back to the striker, from even closer.

Somehow, the keeper threw up a hand, and it flew over the bar.

He later called it the save of his career.

## 8. Penalties

And so the game was going to penalties.

Before the shoot-out, Jamie Carragher reportedly jumped on his goalkeeper's back with some advice.

Put them off, he said. Do what Liverpool's keeper did back in 1984, when they won the European Cup on penalties.

**[On screen: Bruce Grobbelaar · Rome 1984]**

So the keeper danced on his line. Wobbly legs. Arms everywhere.

Milan's first taker blazed it over the bar.

Liverpool scored. Then Milan missed again, saved by the dancing keeper.

Both teams scored. Then Milan's keeper saved one. Then both teams scored again.

**[Shoot-out graphic builds with each line, names under each kick: Serginho ✗ · Hamann ✓ · Pirlo ✗ · Cissé ✓ · Tomasson ✓ · Riise ✗ · Kaká ✓ · Šmicer ✓ · Shevchenko ✗ — final 3–2.]**

Which left Milan's star striker. The European Footballer of the Year. He had to score, or Milan were finished.

The keeper dived, and stopped it with his left hand.

Liverpool were champions of Europe.

## 9. What happened next

It was Liverpool's fifth European Cup. Under the rules of the time, that meant they kept the trophy for good.

They were the last club ever to win the original permanently.

But there was a problem. Liverpool's fifth place in the league meant they weren't in next season's Champions League.

UEFA made an exception. The holders could defend their title, but they had to start in the very first qualifying round.

And that Czech substitute never played for Liverpool again. His penalty in Istanbul was his last kick for the club.

Two years later, the same two teams met in the final again, this time in Athens.

Milan won it, two–one.

But in Liverpool, one word still means more than any other final. Istanbul.
