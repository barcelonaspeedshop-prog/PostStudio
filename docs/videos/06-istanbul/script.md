## 1. Cold open

At half-time in the 2005 Champions League final, Liverpool were losing three–nil against AC Milan, one of the best teams in the world.

Nobody comes back from that. Not in a final, and definitely not against Milan.

Fifteen minutes into the second half, the score was three–three.

This is the story of Liverpool's Champions League miracle in Istanbul.

**[Title card: IMPOSSIBLE. — "Istanbul 2005."]**

## 2. The outsiders

Liverpool shouldn't really have been there in the first place.

They had a new manager, a Spaniard called Rafael Benítez, in his first season at the club.

In the Premier League, they finished fifth. Not even good enough to qualify for the next Champions League.

And in Europe, they nearly went out before Christmas.

In their last group game, at Anfield against Olympiacos, they had to win. And because they'd lost one–nil in Greece, if Olympiacos scored, Liverpool would have to win by two clear goals.

Then the great Rivaldo curled in a free kick. At half-time, Liverpool were losing one–nil. Now they needed three goals in forty-five minutes.

Benítez sent on a young striker, Florent Sinama-Pongolle. Two minutes after the restart, he equalised. One–one.

With nine minutes left, another substitute, Neil Mellor, made it two–one. But that still wasn't enough.

Then, with four minutes to go, Mellor headed the ball down, and Steven Gerrard hit it first time from twenty-five yards, into the corner.

Three–one. Liverpool were through. Looking back, it was a sign of things to come.

After that, they knocked out Bayer Leverkusen, then Juventus, then Chelsea.

And somehow, they were in the final.

## 3. The giants

So to the final. Waiting for Liverpool in Istanbul was a team that can only be described as a European football Goliath. AC Milan.

Champions of Europe two years earlier, and packed with world-class players.

Maldini and Nesta in defence. Pirlo and Kaká in midfield. Shevchenko and Crespo up front.

The final was at the Atatürk Olympic Stadium, on the edge of Istanbul, on the twenty-fifth of May, 2005.

Liverpool in red. Milan in white.

## 4. The first half

It took Milan less than a minute to score.

A free kick came in, and the Milan captain, Paolo Maldini, volleyed it home. About fifty seconds on the clock.

It's still the fastest goal ever scored in a Champions League final.

Then Liverpool lost Harry Kewell to injury. Vladimír Šmicer came on in his place.

Six minutes before the break, Shevchenko crossed, and Hernán Crespo tapped it in. Two–nil.

Right on half-time, Kaká split the Liverpool defence with one pass. Crespo chipped the goalkeeper.

Three–nil to Milan at the break.

## 5. Half-time

Years later, the data company Opta worked out Liverpool's chances from there.

Just over half of one per cent.

**[ODDS graphic: 0.55% — "Opta win probability, 3–0 down".]**

In the dressing room, Benítez didn't panic. He changed the plan.

Dietmar Hamann came on for Steve Finnan. Liverpool switched to three at the back, and Gerrard was pushed further forward.

Meanwhile, up in the stands, the Liverpool fans did something remarkable.

They didn't boo. They sang. "You'll Never Walk Alone."

Some of the players say they could hear it from the dressing room. Benítez says he only heard it on the way back out.

Either way, the fans hadn't given up. So why should the team?

## 6. Six minutes

Nine minutes into the second half, John Arne Riise crossed from the left.

Gerrard rose and headed it in. Three–one.

Two minutes later, Šmicer hit a low shot from outside the box. It skidded past the Milan keeper, Dida. Three–two.

Then Gerrard was brought down in the box by Gennaro Gattuso. Penalty.

Xabi Alonso stepped up. Dida saved it.

But the ball came straight back to Alonso, and he smashed in the rebound.

Three goals in six minutes. Three–three.

**[ODDS graphic: 0.55% → 36.7% — "Opta win probability after Alonso's goal".]**

## 7. The save

After that, Milan took control again. Liverpool clung on into extra time.

With three minutes left, the ball fell to Andriy Shevchenko, right in front of goal.

Liverpool's goalkeeper, Jerzy Dudek, blocked his first effort.

The ball came straight back to Shevchenko, from even closer.

Somehow, Dudek threw up a hand, and it flew over the bar.

Dudek later called it the save of his career.

## 8. Penalties

And so the game was going to penalties.

Before the shoot-out, Jamie Carragher reportedly jumped on Dudek's back with some advice.

Put them off, he said. Do what Bruce Grobbelaar did, when Liverpool won the European Cup on penalties in 1984.

So Dudek danced on his line. Wobbly legs. Arms everywhere.

Serginho went first for Milan. Over the bar.

Hamann scored. Then Dudek saved from Andrea Pirlo.

Cissé scored for Liverpool. Jon Dahl Tomasson scored for Milan.

Dida saved from Riise. Kaká scored. Šmicer scored.

Which left Shevchenko. The reigning European Footballer of the Year. He had to score, or Milan were finished.

Dudek dived, and stopped it with his left hand.

Liverpool were champions of Europe.

## 9. What happened next

It was Liverpool's fifth European Cup. Under the rules of the time, that meant they kept the trophy for good.

They were the last club ever to win the original permanently.

But there was a problem. Liverpool's fifth place in the league meant they weren't in next season's Champions League.

UEFA made an exception. The holders could defend their title, but they had to start in the very first qualifying round.

Šmicer never played for Liverpool again. That penalty was his last kick for the club.

Two years later, the same two teams met in the final again, this time in Athens.

Milan won it, two–one.

But in Liverpool, one word still means more than any other final. Istanbul.
