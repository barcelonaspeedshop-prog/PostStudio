# IMPOSSIBLE: Leicester City, 5000–1 (the detailed cut)

Script for narration, about 1,400 words, roughly 9 minutes at a relaxed pace.

Read it naturally and change any wording that doesn't sound like you. [Square brackets] are notes for the edit; don't read them out.

## 1. Cold open

Five thousand to one.

Those were the odds on Leicester City winning the Premier League in the summer of 2015.

The season before, they'd spent months at the bottom of the league. Nearly everyone expected them to go down.

Nine months later, they were champions of England.

This is the story of how Leicester did the impossible.

## 2. What five thousand to one means

[Animated explainer: the odds board, then a grid of 5,001 dots, one lit.]

So what does five thousand to one actually mean?

It means the bookmakers thought that if you could play the season five thousand and one times, Leicester would win it once.

For comparison, some bookmakers offered two thousand to one on Elvis being found alive.

Bookmakers don't guess. They set odds from money. Big clubs spend the most, and the clubs that spend the most nearly always finish at the top.

[A bar chart of 2015 wage bills, Leicester's tiny bar at the bottom.]

Leicester's whole team reportedly cost less than some clubs paid for one player.

## 3. The great escape

To understand how unlikely it was, go back one season.

In 2014–15, Leicester's first season back in the Premier League, they were bottom at Christmas. They were still bottom at the start of April.

Then they won seven of their last nine games, and stayed up. Football people called it the great escape.

But that summer, the manager who'd saved them left the club.

And Leicester appointed Claudio Ranieri.

## 4. The man nobody wanted

Ranieri was sixty-three. A gentleman, and a good coach. He'd managed Chelsea, Juventus and Valencia.

But he had never won a league title. Not in thirty years.

And his last job had gone badly. He'd been sacked as manager of Greece after they lost at home to the Faroe Islands.

When Leicester announced him, Gary Lineker, a Leicester legend himself, posted two words: Claudio Ranieri? Really?

## 5. The team

Ranieri didn't tear the team up. He looked at what he had.

Up front, Jamie Vardy. Four years earlier he'd been playing non-league football, and working in a factory making medical splints.

[Fact card: Jamie Vardy.]

On the wing, Riyad Mahrez. Leicester had signed him from the French second division for around four hundred thousand pounds.

In goal, Kasper Schmeichel, the son of Manchester United's great Peter Schmeichel.

And in the summer, they signed a quiet midfielder from Caen, in France. N'Golo Kanté.

Hardly anyone in England had heard of him.

## 6. How Leicester played

[Animated pitch explainer: Leicester in blue, the opponents in red.]

So how did a team like that beat the richest clubs in the world?

They didn't try to keep the ball. Leicester had less of it than almost anyone in the league.

Instead, they sat in two banks of four, close together, and waited.

[The two lines of four slide together; the gaps between them close.]

When the other team came forward, there was nowhere to go.

And when Leicester won the ball, they didn't pass it sideways. They went straight for goal.

[The ball goes long, in behind the defence, and Vardy sprints onto it.]

One pass from Kanté or Drinkwater, over the top, and Vardy was gone. Into the space behind a defence that had pushed up to attack.

And Kanté was the key to all of it.

[Kanté's dot races across the pitch, winning the ball three times.]

He seemed to be everywhere. He won the ball back more than anyone in the league that season. People joked that seventy per cent of the Earth was covered by water, and the rest was covered by Kanté.

## 7. The run

Leicester started well, and nobody took them seriously. Surely it wouldn't last.

Then Vardy started scoring. And didn't stop.

He scored in eleven Premier League games in a row. A new record.

[Counter: 1 to 11, the eleventh against Manchester United.]

At Christmas, Leicester were top of the league.

And in February, they went to Manchester City, the richest club in the country, and won three–one.

Ranieri kept things light. He promised the players pizza if they kept a clean sheet. In training, when someone switched off, he'd ring an imaginary bell and say, dilly ding, dilly dong.

The pressure was enormous. Ranieri just kept smiling.

## 8. The night it was decided

[Build-up: the countdown, the two clubs, the players at a house.]

By the end of April, there was only one team that could catch them. Tottenham.

On Monday the second of May, Tottenham went to Chelsea. If they didn't win, Leicester were champions.

The Leicester players watched it together, at Jamie Vardy's house.

At half-time, Tottenham were two–nil up.

Then, in the second half, Chelsea pulled one back.

Seven minutes from the end, the ball fell to Eden Hazard. He curled it into the top corner.

Two–two.

[The house explodes: the players jumping on the sofas.]

At Vardy's house, the room went mad. The players jumped on the sofas, on each other, on anything.

When the final whistle went at Stamford Bridge, Leicester City were champions of England.

Five thousand to one.

## 9. The party

Five days later, Leicester lifted the trophy at the King Power Stadium.

The opera singer Andrea Bocelli sang Nessun Dorma on the pitch, with Ranieri beside him.

[The trophy lift, confetti, the parade bus.]

A week later, a quarter of a million people filled the streets of Leicester for the parade.

They finished with eighty-one points. Ten points clear.

They lost just three games all season.

## 10. What happened next

Riyad Mahrez was voted the players' Player of the Year. Jamie Vardy was the writers' Footballer of the Year.

N'Golo Kanté went to Chelsea that summer, and won the league again the following season. Two titles in two years, with two different clubs.

Leicester played in the Champions League for the first time, and reached the quarter-finals.

And Claudio Ranieri, the man nobody wanted, became one of the most loved managers in English football.

## 11. What if

So here's the question.

Was it Ranieri? Kanté? Vardy's goals? Or was it the season everyone else got it wrong?

Tell me what you think in the comments.

## Fact notes (for us, not for reading)

- **The odds:** 5000–1 pre-season 2015–16 (widely reported). The Elvis 2000–1 comparison was widely reported; keep "some bookmakers".
- **2014–15:** bottom at Christmas and into April (about 140 days bottom). Won 7 of the last 9 games and finished 14th.
- **The manager:** Nigel Pearson left in June 2015. Ranieri was appointed in July 2015, aged 63. He had no top-flight league title before Leicester. Greece lost 0–1 at home to the Faroe Islands in November 2014 and he was sacked. Lineker's tweet: "Claudio Ranieri? Really?"
- **The players:**
  - Vardy: non-league with Stocksbridge Park Steels, Halifax and Fleetwood; worked in a factory making medical splints (his own account); joined Leicester from Fleetwood in 2012.
  - Mahrez: from Le Havre (Ligue 2) in January 2014, about £400,000.
  - Kanté: from Caen in summer 2015, about £5.6m.
  - Schmeichel: son of Peter.
- **The season:**
  - Vardy scored in 11 consecutive PL games, the record (the 11th was v Man Utd, 28 Nov 2015).
  - Top at Christmas 2015. Won 3–1 at Man City in February 2016.
  - The pizza promise and the "dilly ding, dilly dong" bell are widely reported.
- **How they played:** low possession (among the lowest in the league), a 4-4-2 counter-attack. Kanté led the league in tackles and interceptions ("won the ball back more than anyone"; keep it general).
- **The title:** Chelsea 2–2 Tottenham on 2 May 2016. Spurs were 2–0 up at half-time (Kane, Son). Cahill scored 58', then Hazard 83'. The Leicester players watched at Vardy's house.
- **The party:** trophy lifted 7 May v Everton; Bocelli sang Nessun Dorma. The parade on 16 May drew about 240,000 ("a quarter of a million", widely reported).
- **The numbers:** 81 points, 10 clear of Arsenal, 3 defeats.
- **Awards:** Mahrez PFA Player of the Year 2016; Vardy FWA Footballer of the Year 2016; Kanté won the PL with Chelsea in 2016–17. Leicester reached the CL quarter-finals in 2017.
- **Left out on purpose:** the owner's 2018 helicopter crash (no death or injury content, Mike 10-14).
- **Check before recording:** the Mahrez fee (widely reported as about £400,000).
