# BANNED: The Back-Pass

Script for narration, about 650 words, roughly 5 minutes at a relaxed pace.
Read it naturally and change any wording that doesn't sound like you. **[Square brackets]** are notes for the edit. Don't read them out.
After a mistake, say "stop", pause, and restart the whole sentence.

---

## 1. Cold open  [0:00]

There was a time when a team could win a football match without trying to score.

Get one goal ahead, and pass it back to your goalkeeper. He picks it up. Bounces it. Rolls it out. And you pass it straight back to him.

Again. And again. Until the referee blows the final whistle.

It was legal. It was boring. And in 1992, the most boring tactic in football was finally banned.

---

## 2. The problem  [0:35]

For more than a hundred years, a goalkeeper could pick up the ball whenever a teammate passed it to him.

Most of the time, nobody minded.

But as teams perfected the art of defending, the pass back became all too common. One goal up? Kill the game. Pass it back.

---

## 3. Italia 90  [1:00]

The 1990 World Cup in Italy should have been a celebration.

Instead, it's remembered as one of the dullest tournaments ever played.

Fewer goals per game than any World Cup before or since. Teams sat back, defended, and passed it back to the keeper.

In one game, between the Republic of Ireland and Egypt, the Irish goalkeeper Packie Bonner reportedly had the ball in his hands for almost six minutes.

The game finished nil–nil.

Football's lawmakers watched it, and decided something had to change.

---

## 4. The last dance  [1:50]

But before the new rule came in, there was one more tournament. Euro 92, in Sweden.

And the team that won it wasn't even supposed to be there.

Denmark had failed to qualify. Then, with barely ten days to go, Yugoslavia were thrown out because of the war, and Denmark got their place.

They beat France. They beat the Netherlands on penalties. And in the final, they beat Germany two–nil.

How did they pull off the football shock of the century? Well, a big part of it was their keeper, Peter Schmeichel, picking up back-pass after back-pass, as the clock ran down.

It was the last major tournament before the rule changed.

---

## 5. The ban  [2:55]

That summer, the law changed.

If a player deliberately kicks the ball back to his own goalkeeper, the keeper is not allowed to pick it up. If he does, the other team gets a free kick, right there in the penalty area.

Picking up a back-pass. Banned.

---

## 6. Chaos  [3:20]

At first, it was carnage.

Goalkeepers had spent their whole careers using their hands. Now, with a striker charging at them, they had to control the ball with their feet.

Some of them panicked and smashed it into the stands. Some of them miskicked it. Some of them got caught on the ball, and watched it roll into an empty net.

Defenders weren't safe either. For years, a pass back to the keeper had been the safe option. Now it was a risk.

---

## 7. What happened next  [4:05]

But the game got faster. Teams couldn't kill time the same way, so they had to keep playing.

A few years later, throw-ins to the keeper were added to the rule. Then keepers were given just six seconds to release the ball from their hands.

And something nobody expected happened. A whole new kind of goalkeeper appeared.

Keepers who could pass like midfielders. Keepers who start attacks, play out from the back, and come forty yards off their line to sweep up.

Today, a goalkeeper who can't use his feet doesn't get near the top level.

The rule that was meant to stop time-wasting ended up inventing the modern goalkeeper.

---

## 8. Thirty-three years later  [5:00]

And it still isn't over.

In 2025, the lawmakers went further. Hold the ball in your hands for more than eight seconds, and the other team gets a corner.

Packie Bonner's six minutes? These days, he'd have given away a corner before he'd even bounced the ball twice.

---

So here's the question.

Did the back-pass rule save football? Or did it just make goalkeepers' lives a misery?
